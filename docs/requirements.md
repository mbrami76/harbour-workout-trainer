# Requirements: Workout Trainer

Workout Trainer is a workout timer for Sailfish OS. Package name: `harbour-workout-trainer`. Version 1.63.

Verification: **T** host test, **D** device test, **I** inspection. Numbers are never reused; retired requirements stay in the list marked *retired*. Release scope: **V1** unless marked *V2*.

## 1. Purpose and scope

An offline workout timer that guides repetition-based, interval (HIIT) and circuit sessions with a large display and sound cues. It is used anywhere (gym, home, outdoors), so the display must stay legible in varying light. V1 keeps a silent session log. V2 adds history screens, an activity dashboard and voice cues.

### Terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Exercise** | A reusable library entry: name, muscle groups, instructions, form tips. It says *what* the movement is and has no duration. | Push-up |
| **Step** | One period inside a workout. It uses an exercise (work, warm-up, cooldown) or is a rest, and ends by a duration or a repetition count. It says *what, for how long or how many, and when*. The same exercise can appear in many steps. | Push-up 30 s; Push-up x 12; Rest 10 s |
| **Block** | An ordered list of steps repeated for a number of rounds. | A circuit of 4 steps |
| **Round** | One pass through a block. | Round 2 of 3 |
| **Workout** | An ordered list of blocks, saved under a name. | Morning HIIT |
| **Session** | One execution of a workout, from start to end or stop. | Today's Morning HIIT |

There is no separate "set" entity. What a gym calls a set (for instance 12 push-ups) is one repetition step executed once; "3 sets of 12" is a block with that step and 3 rounds. "Interval" in the timer sense means a timed step.

Example: the workout *Morning HIIT* has one block of 3 rounds with the steps Push-up 30 s (work), Rest 10 s, Squat 30 s (work), Rest 10 s. It uses two exercises (Push-up, Squat) in four steps, and one session of it executes 12 steps.

Out of scope for V1: any network function, online exercise database, images of exercises, audio or video demos, user-supplied audio files, operation with the screen off or the app in the background, wearables, history screens, dashboard and voice cues (V2).

## 5. Workout definition

| ID | Requirement | Ver. |
|---|---|---|
| R-WRK-1 | The user can create, edit, duplicate and delete workouts. Lists of workouts are sorted by name without regard to case, and a number in a copy suffix follows the plain copy ("x (copy)" before "x (copy 2)"). | T, D |
| R-WRK-2 | A workout is an ordered list of blocks. A block is an ordered list of steps with a number of rounds (1 or more). | T |
| R-WRK-3 | Step types: work, rest, warm-up, cooldown. Work, warm-up and cooldown steps refer to an exercise; a rest step does not. | T |
| R-WRK-4 | A step ends either by duration (whole seconds) or by repetitions (count). | T |
| R-WRK-5 | A block can define a rest between rounds, and a workout a rest between blocks. | T |
| R-WRK-6 | **Arranging blocks and steps.** In the builder the user can reorder the blocks, reorder the steps inside a block and move a step to another block, by hand and without recreating anything, with buttons that move one place at a time (no drag gesture); a step at the end of a block moves into the neighbouring block, and a block left without steps stays and is reported by the validation (R-WRK-8). A block can be duplicated: the copy is a new block placed after the original, identical in rounds, rests and steps, and as editable as any block (its steps are copies, so changing one does not change the other). Every block can be collapsed to one line (its title, rounds and planned time) and expanded again; while blocks are being arranged (one switch in the builder) they are all shown collapsed and are expanded again when the arranging ends, and the collapsed state is only a view, kept for the open editor and never stored in the workout. | D |
| R-WRK-7 | A circuit is a block whose steps repeat for N rounds. There is no separate quick builder: an interval workout is built like any other, or started from a template (R-TPL-2), and the interval timer mode (R-MOD-1) covers quick work and recovery rounds without preparation. | T |
| R-WRK-8 | Input is validated: durations at least 1 s, repetitions and rounds at least 1, upper limits defined and enforced, and no block without steps in a saved workout. | T |
| R-WRK-9 | The workout shows its total planned time. A paced repetition step (by the rhythm of its exercise or by a timed pace, R-WRK-14) counts with its repetitions times the length of one repetition; a repetition step that ends on a tap is excluded and the display says so. | T |
| R-WRK-10 | **Paced repetition step.** A repetition step that is paced (by the rhythm of its exercise, R-EXR-10, or by a timed pace, R-WRK-14) runs as in R-MET-3 and R-MET-4. The step keeps its own number of repetitions and the session runs them at the pace; the step ends by itself after the last repetition; a rhythm brings no repetitions, sets or rest (the rounds and the rest steps of the workout make the sets and the pauses). Ending the step early needs a long press on "done" (R-TMR-11). The log stores the actual seconds as for any step (R-HST-2). A step that ends by duration is not paced. The step stores nothing about the metronome: if the exercise loses its metronome the step simply runs as a plain repetition step. | T, D |
| R-WRK-11 | Every step has a start mode, chosen in the builder: starts automatically (the default: it begins after the lead-in as soon as the step before it ends) or waits for a tap (the session holds before the step, shows what comes next and the label "tap to start", and begins the lead-in of the step only when the user taps the play button). While the session waits, the play button pulses in green and a sound of its own is played when the wait begins and repeated every 3 s until the user taps (R-ALR-1). The mode is part of the step in storage and in export files. It applies to every step type and to repetition and tempo steps alike, except the first step of the first block, which always starts automatically: the Start tap that opens the session is its trigger, and the builder does not offer the choice for it. | T, D |
| R-WRK-12 | **Workout identity.** Each workout has an icon, chosen from a bundled set of workout icons (more evocative than the exercise icons: gym equipment, cardio and sport, the body, animals, nature and symbols; the default icon stays), and a colour from the palette of R-EXR-8; both have a default and are stored as keys (an unknown key loads with the default shown). Two workouts may have the same name only if their icon or their colour differs. Saving a workout whose name (compared without case and without surrounding blank space), icon and colour are all equal to those of another workout is refused with a clear message and nothing is saved; a duplicate (R-WRK-1) and an import (R-EXC) resolve the clash by the suffix " (copy)", " (copy 2)". Names are compared without case and without surrounding blank space (blank space inside counts); the rule is checked when a record is created and when an edit changes its name, icon or colour; a copy or an imported workout takes the first free suffix for its full identity. Only workouts are exchanged; challenges and their results are not part of an export file. Icon and colour show wherever the workout appears. | T, D |
| R-WRK-13 | **Building without losing input.** While a workout is being built, the user can pick an exercise from the library (searched by name, limited by muscle group); the first choice of the picker is "New exercise", which opens the exercise editor and, when it is saved, returns to the builder with the new exercise selected for that step and with everything else the user had entered intact. Cancelling the editor also returns with the input intact. Leaving the builder with the back gesture drops unsaved changes without asking, as in the other editors. | D |
| R-WRK-14 | **Step ending and pace.** A step of an exercise with a rhythm (R-EXR-10) ends by repetitions only: the user sets the repetitions, the session runs them at the rhythm and the step ends by itself after the last one. A step of an exercise without a rhythm ends by duration or by repetitions; repetitions end on a tap on "done" or are timed by one of two paces, repetitions in a total time or a time per repetition (as R-MOD-7 and R-MOD-8, one beat per repetition), and then the step ends by itself after the last repetition. The pace is part of the step; it is not a saved object. A repetition lasts at least 0.5 s and at most 60 s. A step that ends by duration has no pace, and the core refuses one. A step that already ends by duration stays valid when its exercise later gets a rhythm, so that saved workouts stay editable, and the session runs it by its duration. A pace stored on the step is kept but is neither checked nor used while the exercise has a rhythm, and is used and checked again after the rhythm is detached. | T, D |

## 6. Timer engine

| ID | Requirement | Ver. |
|---|---|---|
| R-TMR-1 | A lead-in countdown (0 to 10 s, default 3 s, configurable) precedes each timed step. | T |
| R-TMR-2 | A timed step counts down and advances automatically at zero. | T |
| R-TMR-3 | A repetition step shows the target count and advances when the user taps the skip button, which on such a step is its "done" (a repetition step with a tempo ends by itself, R-WRK-10, and a tap on the skip button ends the current set early). A long press on the skip button skips to the next block (R-TMR-11). The following rest step is timed. | T, D |
| R-TMR-4 | During a session the user can pause, resume, skip to the next step (a tap) or to the next block (a long press), and stop (R-TMR-11). There is no going back to the previous step. | T, D |
| R-TMR-5 | Timing uses a monotonic clock. The error over a 60 min session is at most 0.1 s. | T |
| R-TMR-6 | The current block, round and step are tracked and shown as position / total. | T |
| R-TMR-7 | While the app is in the foreground, the display is kept on from session start to its end or stop, including while the session is paused. Prevention is released at the end or stop and set again whenever the app returns to the foreground with a session in progress. The app cannot keep the display on in the background. | D |
| R-TMR-8 | When the app becomes inactive for any reason (incoming call answered, power key or display off, gesture, other app), the session pauses. It stays paused when the app is active again (after the call ends or the device is unlocked) and resumes only when the user lifts the pause. | D |
| R-TMR-9 | The pause is triggered by the application's active state, not by page visibility (with the lock screen up the page is visible but the app is inactive; removing a page from the stack changes no app state). If an incoming call does not make the app inactive on a device, call detection is added. | D |
| R-TMR-10 | **Removed in 1.50.** The session screen has no control to add or remove a round or a set; a session runs the saved workout as it is. Adding or removing a set in the metronome stays (R-MET-6). | T, D |
| R-TMR-11 | Every premature end and every change of the plan ahead needs a long press instead of a tap or a confirmation dialog: stopping a session, an interval timer run or a metronome run; skipping to the next block (a tap on the skip button skips to the next step and needs no long press, R-TMR-3); resetting a running or paused countdown or stopwatch, which discards the time reached; and adding or removing a set in the metronome (R-MET-6), for uniformity, whether or not it ends the run. The button must stay pressed for 2 s, a visible progress fills while it is held, and releasing before the end cancels with no effect, so that a stray tap does nothing. The timers and the pace run on while the button is held. Not covered: pause and resume, the tap on the skip button (next step, "done" of a repetition step, R-TMR-3), the stop button of time attack and the stopwatch (their purpose), and reset of a timer that is idle or whose countdown has finished. | D |
| R-TMR-12 | **Exercise file in a session.** While a workout session runs, a step that has an exercise (and the rest or lead-in before such a step, for the next exercise) offers a button that opens the file of the exercise as an overlay on the session screen: its pictogram, name, level colour, muscle groups, rhythm if any, instructions and tips, as in the exercise library. The timer, the voice and the sounds go on while the overlay is open, the remaining time of the step and the pause and stop controls stay visible and usable, and the overlay is closed with a close button or the back gesture. It shows the exercise for which it was opened until it is closed, also if the session moves to the next step. It scrolls when the text does not fit, in both orientations and at the largest system font. A step without an exercise has no button. | D |

## 7. Alerts

| ID | Requirement | Ver. |
|---|---|---|
| R-ALR-1 | Distinct sounds for: work start, rest start, last-seconds ticks, round change, workout complete, and a step that waits for a tap (R-WRK-11). | D |
| R-ALR-2 | Each sound event can be switched on or off. Sounds follow the system volume. There is also a switch for the metronome clicks. The switch of the workout-complete sound also covers the end sounds of a countdown, a stopwatch and a time attack run. The lead-in ticks, the ticks of a rest and the ticks of the timers use the switch and the list of the last-seconds ticks. | D |
| R-ALR-3 | All bundled sounds are original or under a licence compatible with GPL-3.0-or-later, with provenance recorded. | I |
| R-ALR-4 | Optional vibration at step changes through the QtFeedback `ThemeEffect` (the Harbour validator rejects `Nemo.Ngf`). The option is offered only where the platform reports that vibration is supported. | D |
| R-ALR-5 | The app does not accept user-supplied audio files. | I |
| R-ALR-6 | **Spoken announcement.** Before a step that has an exercise, the app speaks: "Get ready to perform <exercise name>", then the number of repetitions or the time of the step, then the description of the exercise. The tips are not spoken (they are read in the exercise file, R-TMR-12). The step timer starts when the last word has been spoken. The pause button stops the voice as it stops the timer, and resuming repeats the announcement from its start. The same announcement is spoken during the rest before the step, so that it is heard before the step begins. The description is spoken only the first time the exercise appears in a run; later the announcement ends after the repetitions or the time. A step whose exercise has no description speaks what it has. The sentences are translatable texts with number forms (R-I18N-1) and are spoken in the language of the exercise (R-EXR-12). | D |
| R-ALR-7 | **Engine and voice.** Speech uses only offline synthesis. The voice is chosen automatically from the language tag of the text; the user does not pick voices one by one. The app has an internal engine with its own voices, and the settings offer to use an engine and voices that are already installed on the device instead of the internal one; the app detects at run time which exist and offers only those. The user can install language packages (R-I18N-6). | D |
| R-ALR-8 | If no engine or no voice for the language of a text is available, that text is skipped silently, the step sound still plays, and the settings say why voice is unavailable. | T, D |
| R-ALR-9 | The fixed cues (R-ALR-1) may be short spoken words in English, produced offline when the app is built with a speech engine whose licence allows redistribution of its output, and bundled as audio files; the provenance (engine, voice, version, licence) is recorded (R-ALR-3). V1 ships English cues only. This is separate from the user-written spoken exercise cues of V2 (R-ALR-6). | I |
| R-ALR-10 | **Preparing the voice.** After Start is pressed the app synthesises the texts of the run in the order in which they will be needed (announcements, descriptions, tips, the rest sentence), once per distinct text and voice, and keeps in memory only the audio that has not been played yet, apart from the short texts that come back (the rest sentence), which stay until the run ends. Audio is never written to storage. Synthesis stays ahead of the speech by at least the whole of the next step, in one background thread with at most two threads of computation. The run starts as soon as the first announcement can start; a progress indication is shown until then, with a way to start without voice. If a text is not ready when it is due, it is skipped silently (R-ALR-8) and the step goes on. | T, D |
| R-ALR-11 | **Rest.** At the beginning of a rest of 5 s or more the app speaks "Now you can rest for N seconds" (N is the length of the rest; a rest of 90 s or more is spoken as minutes and seconds); a shorter rest has no rest sentence. The rest lasts the set time; if the announcement of R-ALR-6 for the next step is still being spoken when the rest ends, the step timer waits for its end (the rest is not shortened or lengthened by the voice). | T, D |
| R-ALR-12 | **Beats in step.** The sound, the visual pulse and the vibration of a beat or a cue are triggered from the same scheduled time of the core (R-TMR-5, R-MET-5), so that none depends on the load of the screen; the visual pulse is started from the scheduled time and not from the moment a signal arrives. There is no calibration setting: the outputs are not shifted against each other. On the target phone, with the speaker and with wired or Bluetooth headphones tested, the sound, the pulse and the vibration of a beat seem simultaneous (device test); headphones with a larger delay are not corrected by the app. | D |
| R-ALR-13 | **What is not spoken.** The description and tips of a rhythm are not spoken; a rhythm step has the announcement of R-ALR-6, the phase names of R-ALR-15 and then its sounds. Quick start speaks "Get ready" and the duration, with no description. | T, D |
| R-ALR-14 | **Pauses in speech.** The voice pauses after the end of every sentence (full stop, question mark, exclamation mark) and, more briefly, after commas, semicolons and colons, and between the head of the announcement and the description. The lengths are named constants of the code, with the first values 600 ms, 300 ms and 600 ms, so that they can be adjusted after a listening test on the phone. | D |
| R-ALR-15 | **Cues spoken during a step.** (a) Steps and timers with a set time and no tempo (workout steps by duration, rests, the interval timer's periods, the countdown): the voice says "Halfway" at the middle of a period of 45 s or more and "Ten seconds left" 10 s before the end of a period of more than 10 s, at the same scheduled times as the halo cues of R-UI-7. (b) Repetition steps with a tempo have neither cue; instead the voice says the repetitions left, "N to go", at the middle repetition (steps of 6 repetitions or more) and "Three to go" before the last three. (c) Phase names of a rhythm are spoken at the start of their phase, in the interface language; only phases the user named are spoken (not "Phase n"). A setting (a single global one) sets how many repetitions have their phase names spoken: none, the first repetition, the first 3 repetitions (the default) or all; for a repetition step the repetitions are counted from the start of the step. Names of phases shorter than 1 s are spoken anyway, and a name still being spoken is cut when the next phase starts. (d) A cue that falls while the voice is speaking an announcement (R-ALR-6, R-ALR-11) is skipped; a phase name takes precedence over the repetitions-left calls of (b): when the call falls while a phase name is being spoken or at the start of a phase whose name is to be spoken, the call is skipped and the name is not cut, and nothing the voice says changes a time. The change applies to this run only; the remaining time and the set position are updated.  The add and remove controls are 2 s holds (R-TMR-11); a line says why one is off (at most 99 sets; no set left to remove). | T, D |
| R-MET-7 | **Saved metronomes.** The user can save metronomes with their settings (the phases with their durations and names), give each a name (a rhythm has no icon and no colour), edit, duplicate and delete them. Two metronomes never have the same name (compared without regard to case and to surrounding blank space), and saving one whose name equals that of another is refused with a clear message; a duplicate takes the suffix " (copy)". The phase names are part of the content of a metronome: two metronomes that differ only in a phase name are different. "Save as new" stores the values shown as a new metronome and is refused, with the reason, while its name equals that of another metronome, the original included; only "save on a copy" (R-MET-8) and duplicate take the suffix. Trying from the editor runs the values shown, saved or not, and writes nothing. Leaving the editor with the back gesture drops unsaved changes without asking. | T, D |
| R-MET-8 | **Metronomes and exercises.** A change to a saved metronome changes the tempo of every exercise linked to it. Before saving a change to a metronome that exercises use, the app tells the user how many and which exercises are affected and offers: save the change on a copy (the original and its exercises unchanged), save anyway, or cancel. Deleting a metronome that exercises use asks for confirmation naming the exercises; when the user confirms, the metronome is deleted and its links are cleared in one operation (no exercise is left pointing at it, in the stored data as in the app), and the exercises keep all their data and only lose the tempo. | T, D |
| R-MET-9 | **Naming of rhythms.** A rhythm that is shipped with the app, and any rhythm the user wants to describe in the same way, has a name that starts with a short code of its phases, followed by a label. The code is the number of phases and the letter P, then the durations in seconds separated by hyphens: `4P-U-4` is four phases of the same length (U for uniform) that add up to 4 s; `3P-3-1-1` is three phases of 3 s, 1 s and 1 s; `2P-U-1` is two phases of 0.5 s. A uniform rhythm names the total, and the phases must come out in steps of 0.5 s (R-MET-2). From five phases on, the durations may be replaced by the length of every phase and a short name, `<n>P-<duration>-<Name>`. The code is part of the name text, not a separate field. | I |
